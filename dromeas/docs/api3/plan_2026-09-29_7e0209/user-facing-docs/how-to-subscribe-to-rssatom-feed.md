# How to subscribe to the blog's RSS or Atom feed

You can follow the blog with your favorite feed reader and get new posts automatically, without visiting the website every day.

## Before you start

- You need a feed reader, such as a built-in browser feed, a mobile news app, or an RSS extension.
- You need access to the blog page on the website.

## Get the feed address

1. Open the **Blog** page in your web browser.
2. Look for a feed or subscribe icon in the address bar or on the page. The blog publishes two feed formats:
   - **RSS** — a widely supported feed format
   - **Atom** — an alternative feed format with the same post content

   Both formats provide the same blog posts. Pick whichever your reader supports.
3. If your browser does not show a feed icon, open the blog's feed address directly. The RSS feed address ends in `rss.xml`. The Atom feed address ends in `atom.xml`.

## Subscribe in your feed reader

4. Open your feed reader app.
5. Choose **Add feed**, **Subscribe**, or the **+** button.
6. Paste the feed address you found in step 3.
7. Confirm the subscription.

## Expected result

Your feed reader now shows the blog's recent posts. When a new blog post is published, it appears in your reader automatically.

By default, the feed includes the 20 most recent published posts. Draft posts and unlisted posts are not included.

## Common issues

| Issue | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Your reader says the feed cannot be found | Confirm the address ends exactly in `rss.xml` or `atom.xml`, and make sure the blog has at least one published post. Feeds are only created when blog posts exist. |
| New posts do not appear immediately | Check your feed reader's refresh or sync interval. Some readers update only every few hours. |
| Older posts are missing | The default feed shows only the most recent 20 posts. Subscribe promptly to stay current or check the blog page for older posts. |
| The feed looks empty or incomplete in your reader | Verify that your reader supports the feed format. Some older readers work better with `rss.xml` than `atom.xml`. |
